About the Diocese
of Enugu

A brief history, our leadership, the work of the Chancery, and the wider diocesan family that makes up this vibrant Church.

A Brief History

From 17 parishes in 1962
to 248 today.

The Catholic Diocese of Enugu was raised as an independent See on November 12, 1962, carved out of the Onitsha Archdiocese. From 17 original parishes it has grown into one of the most vibrant dioceses in Nigeria — encompassing 2,738 square kilometres of territory and serving over 1.4 million Catholics.

The Diocese operates a network of hospitals, schools, vocational centres, and is presently undertaking the reconstruction of the iconic Holy Ghost Cathedral in Enugu — a monumental project of worship befitting the Cathedral Church of the state capital.

Under episcopal leadership renewed each generation, the Diocese continues the work of the Gospel across Enugu State. Today, 531 diocesan priests, 669 religious women, 168 major seminarians, and a vibrant lay apostolate serve every corner of the territory.

1962
Diocese Established
Erected as a separate See on November 12, 1962, carved out of the Archdiocese of Onitsha, with 17 original parishes.
1962
First Bishop Installed
Most Rev. Godfrey Mary Paul Okoye, C.S.Sp., became the first Bishop of Enugu.
1996
A New See
Most Rev. Anthony Okonkwo Gbuji appointed Bishop of Enugu; installed February 8, 1997.
2009
Fifth Bishop
Most Rev. Callistus Valentine Chukwuma Onaga installed as fifth Bishop of Enugu on May 2, 2009.
2018
Auxiliary Bishop
Most Rev. Ernest Anezichukwu Obodo ordained Auxiliary Bishop on August 31, 2018 — the first in the See's history.
Today
248 Parishes, 6 Deaneries
A thriving diocese of 531 priests, 669 religious women, 168 seminarians, and 4 diocesan hospitals.
